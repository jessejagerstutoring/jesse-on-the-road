![The Jam hostel, narrow and tucked away.](placeholder-3x2)

A few more days, and many adventures, later. I took it easy for the first couple of days, but I soon ended up at one of the best hostels I have ever stayed in. It was a fairly narrow place, tucked away behind the bustle of Bangkok. It was close to all the backpackers, of course, so it didn't take long to make some friends.

After checking in at the Jam hostel, I went for a walk with two guys from America and New Zealand, and we did what backpackers do: we got a toastie from 7-Eleven.

And oh man, those 7-Elevens. They are a bit like a Dutch AH to go, and they are truly eeeeverywhere. Sometimes directly opposite each other.

The walk didn't last long, and I figured I would catch some extra sleep and crawl into bed.

Back in the room, a new roommate soon wandered in and asked if I felt like exploring the city centre. He turned out to be from Malta, travelling with a good friend from England. A few short days later I decided not to carry on with them. He was mostly after a woman at the hostel, and did everything he could to spend as much time with her as possible.

***

The nice thing was that someone else was sleeping in my room too: a guy a bit older than me, from South Korea. He had cared for his sick mother, who sadly passed away in the end, and had then decided to go travelling.

I have to say, our first meeting was rather strange. He came into the room blind drunk to announce that he had lost his phone, and then promptly pulled a phone out of his bag.

> It turned out to be a different phone.

The next morning, in pure desperation, he tried calling the other one, and he got lucky. Someone actually answered, and was even willing to bring it back. So I figured I'd wait with him. A few hours later someone turned up on a tuk-tuk with the phone (for a fee, naturally). Anyway, we could move on.

![A street of stalls, where every metre comes with an invitation.](placeholder-16x9)

Our plan was to head into the city. I say that now, but going into town works a bit differently in Asia than it does back in Europe. A city of little streets and pavements isn't really a thing here. Either you go to a street lined with stalls, or you go to a shopping mall that could swallow five Dutch shopping centres. We chose the first.

Once there, we were called inside at every metre. That feels awkward and strange at first, but after a while you get used to turning people down.

At some point we found a nice spot for a drink and some food (though in Thailand you can eat practically anywhere, toilets included, so to speak). We got talking to the person next to us, an American who, after 15 years in the army, was going back and forth between Asia and America. I have to say, he told us he was 38, but judging by his behaviour and looks, he seemed closer to 27.

Anyway, our club of three went on to have plenty of adventures over the next few days. Too many to list here. Let's just say that Thai customs are a little looser, and that you shouldn't walk down the wrong streets.

::: Field notes
- The hostel: Jam, narrow, hidden, and close to the backpackers.
- 7-Eleven is everywhere and does a proper backpacker's toastie.
- You will be called inside at every metre. You get used to saying no.
- Going into town here means either a street of stalls or a giant mall.
