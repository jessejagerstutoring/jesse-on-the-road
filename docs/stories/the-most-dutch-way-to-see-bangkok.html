The Most Dutch Way to See Bangkok

A bike tour through alleys, temples and someone's living room, followed by a rooftop bar and a very expensive glass of wine.

Soon after landing in Bangkok, I knew I wanted to see the city in a fun way. And one of the things people kept recommending was thoroughly Dutch: a bike tour through all of Bangkok's tiny back streets.

When I arrived at Co van Kessel's bike tour, I met, of course, nothing but Dutch people. How could it be otherwise? We were hoisted onto our saddles in no time, and off we went, straight into the chaos of Chinatown.

The route took us through little streets, alleyways and plantations, and onto several boats. We passed plenty of places where tourists would normally never end up: the smallest neighbourhoods, the most hidden temples, the tiniest schools.

Eventually we arrived at a local restaurant. Or rather, simply at someone's home, for a typical Thai meal. It was delicious, of course. It was also, of course, quite spicy.

With full stomachs we were soon back on our bikes, riding on the motorway, along the railway tracks and through the fields, all the way back to where we started.

* * *

There was plenty of chatting along the way, and I arranged with someone to visit one of the city's many rooftop bars afterwards. The one we picked was even used in the film The Hangover (perhaps better known among the younger crowd).

On arrival I first had to buy a pair of long trousers, far too expensive and of the absolute lowest quality. Anyway, once we got up there, we were received like emperors, with five hosts and hostesses coming over to greet us.

We were received like emperors. A little later we understood why.

A glass of wine easily cost around 1,100 baht, roughly 26 euros. And that's in a city where a beer usually costs 60 to 100 baht.

Quite the shock. But a quick look through the menu turned up the very cheapest whiskey, so we could still enjoy the view for a while. And in the end, it was all worth it.

Field notes

  • The tour: Co van Kessel's bike tour, through Chinatown and on to places tourists rarely see.
  • Bring long trousers to a rooftop bar, or expect to buy a terrible pair on the spot.
  • A beer usually costs 60 to 100 baht. A rooftop glass of wine can cost 1,100.
  • Check the menu for the cheapest whiskey. The view is the same.
